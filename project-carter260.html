Carter Fusion 260 conceptual business jet

A nine-person team design exercise: take two real mid-size business jets apart on paper, and synthesize a higher-capacity, ultra-long-range aircraft from what each does best.

The brief

The Carter Fusion 260 is a conceptual modification of the Bombardier Challenger 650 and Gulfstream G280 — the Challenger contributing cabin volume and range, the G280 contributing short-field performance and efficiency. The target: a Mobile Corporate Office capable of carrying 16 passengers and 4 crew intercontinentally, non-stop.

The structural philosophy borrows from the Boeing 787: CFRP composite construction in place of the baseline aircrafts’ predominantly aluminum structure, chosen for strength-to-weight ratio, fatigue life, and the higher cabin pressure differential it allows.

Design targets

  • MTOW27,295 kg
  • Range7,860 km (4,244 nm)
  • Fuel capacity9,079 kg
  • Payload16 passengers + 4 crew
  • Aerodynamic quality (Kcr)16
  • Powerplant2 × Pratt & Whitney PW814GA/D turbofans

CAD model

Full-aircraft assembly modeled in CATIA V5: fuselage, wing, vertical and horizontal stabilizers, engines, and landing gear as separate parts in one product tree.

Mass & balance analysis

The aircraft's statistical sizing and mass-balance work — wing, tail and fuselage geometry, component-by-component mass estimation, and center-of-gravity balancing across four loading cases — was carried out in Excel, iterating against the mission requirements until the numbers converged on something structurally sound.

Component breakdown of the 27,295 kg MTOW:

  • Wing8,588 kg
  • Fuel9,079 kg
  • Payload3,100 kg
  • Powerplant2,102 kg
  • Equipment2,451 kg
  • Fuselage1,483 kg
  • Landing gear1,056 kg
  • Control surfaces869 kg

Center of gravity

Balancing coefficient calculated across four loading conditions, referenced to the median aerodynamic chord:

  • Full aircraft0.251
  • No load0.236
  • No fuel0.255
  • Empty0.293

Final year thesis · AFIT Kaduna, 2026

Asymmetric aerodynamic-structural analysis of the ATR 72-600 using the actuator disk method

A full-aircraft CFD study of what happens to the airframe when one engine fails during a turboprop climb — run at a fraction of the computational cost of prior high-fidelity methods.

Manuscript in preparation for journal submission

The problem

Turboprop aircraft undergo complex propeller slipstream interactions during critical flight phases such as one-engine-inoperative (OEI) conditions, and characterizing these interactions accurately matters for structural design. High-fidelity unsteady studies can resolve this in detail, but the computational cost — thousands of CPU hours — is often impractical for structural engineers who need aerodynamic load data quickly.

This project applies the steady-state Actuator Disk Method (ADMv2), a formulation previously validated on partial or simplified geometries, to a full production airframe: the ATR 72-600 wing-fuselage-nacelle-empennage assembly, under both symmetric take-off and OEI climb.

Method, in brief

  • SolverANSYS Fluent, RANS with k−ω SST turbulence closure
  • Propeller modelADMv2 — total-pressure boundary condition at the disk face, derived from BEM theory
  • GeometryFull wing-fuselage-nacelle-empennage assembly, built in CATIA V5
  • MeshPoly-hexcore, 616,523 elements / 2,199,109 nodes, grid-independence verified (GCI < 5%)
  • Structural linkOne-way FSI to ANSYS Mechanical for load transfer

Key results

Two flight conditions were simulated: symmetric take-off (both engines at full power) and one-engine-inoperative climb (one disk feathered, zero thrust).

−6,752 Pa Peak upper-surface suction, operative wing root under OEI
35.9 m²/s² Peak turbulence kinetic energy at the operative nacelle-wing junction
~99 m/s Local slipstream velocity on the operative side vs. 59.1 m/s freestream

Symmetric take-off

Both engines at full power produced a mirrored suction and pressure-recovery pattern at each engine-wing junction, with a peak leading-edge stagnation pressure of about 3,300 Pa — a stable, symmetric baseline before the failure case.

One-engine-inoperative

With one disk feathered, the asymmetry propagates across the full airframe: the operative wing root carries severe localized suction while the opposite wing shows a clean, undisturbed pressure gradient — the direct aerodynamic source of the rolling moment and wing-root torsion the central attachment box must carry.

Why the efficiency matters

The full-aircraft simulation converged for both flight conditions in approximately 3 CPU hours. Prior steady-ADM validation work (Yang et al., 2024) required about 170 CPU hours on a 42-million-cell grid, and fully resolved unsteady simulation (Zhao et al., 2022) is understood to need several thousand CPU hours for a comparable domain. The result is a workflow that delivers structurally relevant, asymmetric aerodynamic load data on hardware and license tiers a typical university aerospace department can actually access.

  • This study~3 CPU hours, full-aircraft geometry, both flight conditions
  • Yang et al. (2024)~170 CPU hours, 42M-cell grid, partial geometry
  • Zhao et al. (2022)Several thousand CPU hours, unsteady URANS sliding mesh
